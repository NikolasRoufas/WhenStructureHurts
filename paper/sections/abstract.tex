\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented systems increasingly turn retrieved passages into sentence or claim lists, reasoning orders and relation graphs before generation. Such transformations can help, but they can also make evidence harder to read, spend context on formatting, or assert relations that the sources do not support. System-level comparisons cannot separate these effects, because retrieval, content and formatting change together.

We hold the evidence fixed. Gold supporting passages for multi-hop questions are presented in seven representations whose fact sets are verified identical per question; a second protocol fixes a fact ranking and a token budget. With Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, we tested six contrasts, specified before data collection, on a disjoint sample of 4{,}976 MuSiQue and 2WikiMultiHopQA questions. Graph-derived ordering and explicit derived relation lines had no detectable effect on exact match; on 2WikiMultiHopQA both are equivalent to zero within $\pm2$ points. Splitting passages into sentence items lowered 2WikiMultiHopQA exact match by 1.7 points (95\% CI $[-2.9, -0.5]$), almost entirely through more abstention.

Exploratory controls, added after this test, show where structure hurts. With order and line count held fixed, showing the \texttt{CONTRADICTS} relations predicted by an NLI classifier lowered exact match by 3.3 points on both datasets, mainly by making the reader abstain. The effect replicated on 1{,}000 fresh questions with real retrieval. Without the instruction that permits abstention, most representation differences shrank toward zero. For this reader, the measurable cost of structure came from asserted relations and from abstention behaviour, not from the reorganisation of facts.
\end{abstract}
